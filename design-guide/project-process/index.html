From consultation to handover

A project progresses through decisions, information and approvals. The precise sequence depends on its size, complexity and agreed scope.

From consultation to coordinated design

1 Consultation and project brief

The team discusses your needs, priorities, budget and expectations. A family may focus on privacy and future flexibility; a developer may focus on user needs, unit mix and maintainability.
Typical outcome: A written brief and service proposal.
Client decision: Confirm scope, responsibilities, communication and fee terms before appointment.

2 Site understanding and feasibility

Available records, measurements, site conditions and constraints are reviewed. Surveys, structural advice or other investigations may be needed. Relevant permission requirements should be checked for the specific project.
Typical outcome: A record of constraints, opportunities and information still required.
Client decision: Confirm which investigations and specialist inputs to commission.

3 Concept design

Layouts and an initial design direction are developed around the brief. Options can be tested for circulation, furniture, light, privacy and broad budget fit.
Typical outcome: Concept plans and appropriate explanatory visuals.
Client decision: Select a direction and record revisions before progressing.

4 Design development and coordination

The chosen concept becomes more specific. Architecture, interiors, structure and services are coordinated as relevant. Material choices and cost expectations are reviewed together.
Typical outcome: Developed layouts, key details and an updated cost view.
Client decision: Agree major materials, systems and priorities before detailed documentation.

5 Permissions and detailed documentation

Where permissions are required, establish who prepares, submits and follows up the relevant documents. Submission requirements and construction information serve different purposes. Detailed drawings and specifications define the work for pricing and execution.
Typical outcome: Required submission information and coordinated construction documents within the appointment.
Client decision: Resolve outstanding selections and confirm the basis for tender or quotation.

Some stages overlap. Authority reviews, client decisions and consultant inputs can affect the programme. Do not assume concept approval is permission to begin work.

From contractor selection to handover

Clear responsibility and timely decisions help carry the design through construction.

6 Contractor quotations and selection

Invite quotations against a consistent scope, drawing set and specification. Compare inclusions, exclusions, quantities, material standards, timing and payment terms. A lower total may reflect missing items rather than a better offer.
Typical outcome: A clarified scope and selected contractor.
Client decision: Agree the contract, responsibilities and process for variations before work starts.

7 Execution and design coordination

The contractor organises site labour, methods, procurement and execution under the contract. The design team may respond to queries, review samples and visit at agreed stages. Specialist reviews should be arranged where relevant.
Typical outcome: Recorded clarifications, approved selections and progress reviews within each appointment.
Client decision: Respond to time-sensitive questions and authorise changes only after understanding cost and schedule implications.

8 Completion review and snagging

Review the completed work against the agreed scope. Record incomplete items, visible defects and required corrections with an owner and target date for each. Arrange relevant system testing and demonstrations with the responsible parties.
Typical outcome: A snag list and follow-up record.
Client decision: Confirm what remains open and how it affects occupation or final payments under the contract.

9 Handover and use

Collect keys, manuals, warranties, care instructions and available as-built records as agreed. Understand the location of service controls, how to maintain finishes and whom to contact about defects.
Typical outcome: A documented handover with outstanding obligations recorded.
Client decision: Acknowledge received items and agree follow-up arrangements.

Keep one shared decision record

Record the current drawing revision, approved materials, changes, responsible person and required response date. For developers managing repeated units, clearly identify which changes apply to a prototype, a particular unit or the whole project.

Site visits are not the same as continuous site supervision. Procurement, project management, payment certification and post-handover support should each be confirmed in the written scope. Ask Lynarch Studio to explain the proposed responsibilities for your project.
