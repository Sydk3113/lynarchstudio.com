When to engage an architect

Bring design thinking into the project before the major choices become difficult to reverse.

Before buying a plot or committing to a property

A preliminary design review can help test whether your intended use appears practical for the site or existing building. Discuss access, orientation, surroundings, approximate space needs and known constraints. This review is separate from legal title checks, measured surveys, structural investigations and formal permission advice, which require the appropriate professionals.

Before fixing a new building layout

Engage the design team while the brief and budget are still being shaped. Early work can compare alternatives, establish the consultant inputs needed and identify decisions that affect later stages. A plan chosen only because it fits a preferred facade may be harder to adapt to the family or development brief.

Before apartment interiors begin

Start before electrical points, plumbing changes, ceiling levels and joinery are finalised. Bring the available builder drawings, possession expectations and any building management requirements. Verify site measurements and existing services before releasing work for manufacture or installation.

Before renovating an occupied home

A renovation begins with understanding what already exists. Discuss the condition of the building, known leaks, service routes, what must remain and whether you will live on site. Opening up concealed work can reveal problems, so scope and contingency should reflect uncertainty. Changes affecting structure need appropriate engineering review before work begins.

If construction has already started

Design support can still be useful. Begin with a status review: completed work, current drawings, orders placed, unresolved decisions and the cost of possible changes. The practical next step may be to improve remaining decisions rather than redesign everything.

Bring these to the first conversation

Share the location, available drawings or dimensions, photographs, intended use, approximate overall budget and desired completion window. Add a short list of must-haves and concerns. Developers should also describe intended users, unit mix and sales or rental objectives. Reference images help most when you explain what you like about them.

You do not need a finished brief to start. A useful first conversation helps you discover which questions need answers.
