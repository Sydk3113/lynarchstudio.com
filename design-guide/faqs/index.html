Practical answers for homeowners and property developers.

Questions before you start

Is an architect only for a large or luxury project?

No. The useful question is how much design and coordination your project needs. A compact home can involve difficult decisions about privacy, storage and daylight. Match the appointment to the complexity and intended outcome.

Should I appoint an architect before buying land?

Early design advice can help test the practical fit of your brief. It does not replace legal due diligence, surveys or specialist investigations. Agree exactly what a preliminary review will assess.

Do I need both architecture and interior design?

Some projects benefit from both, and a single practice may provide them. A new building needs the shell and interior requirements to work together. For a limited interior refresh, the required scope may be smaller.

How are professional fees decided?

Fees depend on the proposed services and project particulars. Request a written proposal covering deliverables, stages, revisions, visits, exclusions, payment terms and applicable taxes. Compare the service scope as well as the amount.

Can design reduce the cost of my project?

Design can help clarify priorities, compare options and identify some clashes before construction. It cannot guarantee a saving. Site conditions, market prices, changes and execution all affect the final cost.

How long will my project take?

The programme depends on scope, decisions, permissions where applicable, consultant inputs, procurement and construction conditions. Ask for a stage-based programme and update it when assumptions change rather than relying on one early completion date.

Can the design accommodate Vastu preferences?

Share your preferences at the briefing stage. Discuss how they interact with site constraints, circulation, daylight and other priorities. Record the agreed balance before the layout is developed.

Questions during the project

Will the architect obtain every permission?

Permission-related services depend on the appointment and the project. Confirm who prepares, submits and follows up each requirement. The relevant authority determines the outcome; a design appointment cannot guarantee approval.

Will someone be on site every day?

Do not assume this. Periodic design reviews differ from continuous supervision. Ask who will manage daily execution, how often designers will visit and which specialist checks are included.

Can I use my own contractor?

Discuss this before appointment. The team needs clear arrangements for drawings, queries, samples, site access and changes. Contractor selection should consider relevant capability and the agreed scope, not only familiarity or price.

Can I change my mind once work starts?

Often, but a change may affect cost, lead times and completed work. Ask for the implications in writing before approving it. Some changes may need fresh technical review or permission-related checks.

Can we renovate while living in the home?

It depends on the work, phasing and safe access to essential services. Discuss dust, noise, isolation of work areas and temporary arrangements with the project team. Some scopes may require you to move out for part or all of the work.

What should I receive at handover?

The contract and appointments should define this. Typical items include a snag record, keys, relevant manuals and warranties, maintenance instructions and agreed records of completed work. Identify any outstanding items and who will close them.

What changes for a property developer?

Define intended users, unit mix, operations, maintenance and commercial objectives early. Coordinate repeating details and shared services carefully. Design can support a coherent product, but it cannot guarantee selling prices, rental returns or sales speed.

How do I start a conversation with Lynarch Studio?

Share your project location, type, approximate area, available drawings, broad budget and intended timeline. Tell us what matters most and where you need clarity. These details provide a useful starting point for discussing the right scope.
