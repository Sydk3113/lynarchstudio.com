Clear roles support a coordinated fit-out

Commercial projects often involve several organisations. Define who supplies information, who approves decisions and who executes each item.

Client or operator

Typical contribution
Business brief, equipment needs, budget and timely decisions.
Confirm in writing
Decision-maker, sign-off stages and responsibility for business readiness.

Architect or interior designer

Typical contribution
Spatial design, design documentation and coordination within scope.
Confirm in writing
Deliverables, revisions, visits, tender support and exclusions.

Services and other specialists

Typical contribution
Relevant technical design such as structure, electrical, HVAC, fire systems, kitchen or acoustics.
Confirm in writing
Design boundaries, required inputs, testing and review responsibilities.

Contractor and vendors

Typical contribution
Construction, fabrication, installation and contracted site supervision.
Confirm in writing
Specifications, shop drawings, quality checks, sequencing and warranties.

Landlord or building manager

Typical contribution
Base-building information and building-specific fit-out coordination.
Confirm in writing
Existing provisions, permitted interfaces and review process.

Who coordinates the interfaces

Nominate a lead and agree how information moves between teams. A ceiling can involve lighting, air-conditioning, sprinklers, signage and access panels. Each specialist may own a different system, but their information must fit the same physical space.

What site reviews cover

Periodic design visits differ from continuous supervision. Agree who checks dimensions, workmanship, specialist installation and concealed work before it is covered. Record review points and unresolved items rather than assuming a visit covers every aspect of the project.

If one provider offers design and execution

Combined services can simplify some communication, but the scope still needs to identify design responsibility, cost approvals, execution checks and change control. Ask how conflicts, variations and independent specialist inputs will be handled.

Commercial licensing, landlord consent, technical design and contractor execution are distinct responsibilities. Identify the appropriate party for each before work begins.

Planning a commercial project?

Share the location, business type, approximate area, budget and intended opening date. Include any plans or equipment requirements you already have.
