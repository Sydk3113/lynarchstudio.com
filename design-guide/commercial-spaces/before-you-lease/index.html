Start before you commit to the premises

The right location still needs to be the right physical fit for the business.

Early design advice is most useful before a lease is signed or a purchase is finalised. A preliminary review can identify questions about layout, services and fit-out feasibility while you still have options. It complements legal, commercial and technical due diligence; it does not replace them.

Understand the space you are taking

Request available plans and verify important dimensions on site. Check clear heights, columns, levels, entrances, toilets and existing service locations. Identify the intended use and establish what project-specific permissions and specialist reviews may be needed. Ask the owner or building manager for relevant records and fit-out conditions.

Check the services against your operation

Clarify the available electrical provision, cooling arrangements, ventilation options, water, drainage, data connectivity and equipment locations. For food businesses, establish whether extraction and associated service routes are feasible and acceptable to the building. Do not rely on a previous tenant’s use as proof that your proposal will work.

Make landlord and tenant responsibilities visible

Record who provides each element, who may alter it and who maintains it. Discuss signage, facade changes, working hours, delivery access, loading restrictions, fit-out approvals and reinstatement obligations. Have lease terms reviewed by the appropriate adviser before relying on them.

For an existing business

Review what currently works and where operations struggle. Decide whether renovation can be phased without compromising safe access, essential services or customer experience. Some activities may require temporary closure or relocation. Include the operational consequences in the project plan.

Bring a useful brief

Share the business type, location, available area, expected staff and visitor patterns, operating hours, key equipment, budget and intended opening window. Include brand references and priorities. For landlords and developers, add target tenant types and the proposed division between shell work and fit-out.

An early review should end with a clear list of opportunities, constraints, missing information and decisions needed before commitment.

Planning a commercial project?

Share the location, business type, approximate area, budget and intended opening date. Include any plans or equipment requirements you already have.
