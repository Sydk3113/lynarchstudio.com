From business brief to opening

The stages below form a typical framework. The actual sequence depends on the building, use and agreed appointments.

1 Define the brief and scope

Agree the business objectives, users, activities, budget and opening expectations. Establish deliverables, consultant inputs, site review arrangements and exclusions in writing. Outcome: a brief and a responsibility map.

2 Survey and assess feasibility

Review dimensions, existing conditions, service capacities and landlord constraints. Identify project-specific approval and specialist inputs. Outcome: a feasibility review and an action list for unresolved information.

3 Develop the concept

Test layouts, customer and staff movement, furniture and equipment. Develop the atmosphere and material direction alongside budget assumptions. Outcome: an agreed design direction and recorded client decisions.

4 Coordinate and document

Bring architecture, interiors and relevant technical systems together. Prepare drawings and specifications within scope. Coordinate equipment information, signage and specialist interfaces. Outcome: information suitable for the agreed pricing and execution stages, plus required submissions where commissioned.

5 Compare quotations and plan procurement

Compare offers against the same scope and clarify exclusions, delivery periods and installation responsibilities. Confirm which items control the programme. Outcome: agreed work packages, procurement decisions and a realistic sequence.

6 Execute and review

The contractor manages the contracted execution. Designers and specialists review within their appointments, respond to queries and record changes. Outcome: coordinated decisions, inspection records where applicable and an updated cost and programme position.

7 Test complete and hand over

Arrange relevant system testing and demonstrations, record snags and collect agreed manuals, warranties and completed-work records. Confirm outstanding actions, staff familiarisation and operational readiness. Required permissions and authorisations remain separate from a visual handover review.

Treat the opening date as a coordinated milestone. Build in time for decisions, external reviews, procurement, commissioning and operational setup instead of assuming construction completion alone means the business can open.
