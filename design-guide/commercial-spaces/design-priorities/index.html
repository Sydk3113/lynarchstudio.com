Different businesses need different spaces

Begin with the activities that make your business work, then develop the atmosphere around them.

Offices and workplaces

Plan for actual work patterns: focused tasks, confidential calls, collaboration, meetings and visitors. Balance workstation numbers with circulation, storage, pantry use and support spaces. Coordinate lighting, glare control, acoustics, power and data. A higher desk count is only useful if the space still works for the team.

Retail shops and showrooms

Think through arrival, browsing, product comparison, staff assistance and payment. Test display layouts with real merchandise dimensions. Include replenishment routes, packaging, secure stock and staff needs. Lighting should suit the products and the viewing task. Keep the storefront and signage strategy consistent with the brand and the building constraints.

Cafés and restaurants

Start with the menu, service model and equipment schedule. Dining capacity must be considered alongside preparation, washing, storage, staff circulation and delivery collection. Involve appropriate kitchen and services specialists early to review extraction, make-up air, drainage, equipment loads and other technical needs. A dining layout alone does not establish that a premises can support food operations.

Salons studios and customer service interiors

Map the customer journey from reception to consultation, treatment or service, payment and exit. Consider privacy, waiting time, storage, cleaning and staff movement. Confirm equipment-specific water, power and ventilation needs. For clinics or other specialised uses, involve the relevant sector specialists; a general fit-out guide does not replace their requirements.

Commercial buildings and developer projects

For new buildings, bring architecture, structure and services together from the outset. For multi-tenant premises, define the base-building provision and what future tenants must supply. Review access, service distribution, tenant flexibility, shared areas, maintenance and servicing. Avoid assuming that a generic shell will suit every tenant type without adaptation.

The best starting brief describes a working day: who arrives, what they need, where staff move and what must happen behind the scenes.

Planning a commercial project?

Share the location, business type, approximate area, budget and intended opening date. Include any plans or equipment requirements you already have.
