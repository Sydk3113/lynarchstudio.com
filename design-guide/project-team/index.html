Architect, contractor and interior designer

Each role contributes different expertise. The strongest team makes the interfaces between them explicit.

Architect

Typical focus
Overall spatial design, building form, site response and design coordination within scope.
Clarify before appointment
Deliverables, consultant coordination, permissions support and site review frequency.

Interior designer

Typical focus
Interior planning, finishes, lighting intent, joinery, furniture and the experience of using rooms.
Clarify before appointment
Detailing, services coordination, procurement, installation reviews and exclusions.

Contractor

Typical focus
Pricing and executing the agreed work, organising labour, materials and construction methods.
Clarify before appointment
Scope, specifications, programme, supervision, quality responsibilities and variation terms.

Specialist consultants

Typical focus
Structural or building services design and other specialist advice where needed.
Clarify before appointment
Design responsibility, information required, review stages and fees.

Can one practice provide more than one role

Yes, services may overlap or be combined. An architectural practice may provide interior design; a design-and-build arrangement may combine design and execution. The label alone does not tell you who is responsible for each task. Ask for a clear written scope and understand how design decisions, costs and site quality will be reviewed.

Who should you approach first

For a new building, extension or substantial spatial change, start with the design and technical expertise appropriate to the work. For an interior fit-out within a fixed shell, an interior design lead may be suitable, with architectural or engineering input where the changes require it. For clearly defined repair work, a competent specialist contractor may be the first contact.

Who checks whether the work is correct

The contractor remains responsible for its contracted execution. Designers and engineers review matters within their appointments; their visits do not automatically replace day-to-day site supervision. Agree who checks dimensions, workmanship, specialist installations and completed work, and how issues are recorded.

Ask every proposed team member the same question: What will you deliver, what will you review and what remains someone else’s responsibility?
