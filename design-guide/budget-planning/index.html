Planning a realistic project budget

Begin with the total amount you are prepared to commit, then decide what the project must include within it.

A construction or interior quotation may cover only part of the money you will spend. Build a project budget that makes the boundaries visible. Separate confirmed commitments from allowances and optional upgrades, and update the budget as the design becomes more detailed.

Treat an early estimate as a planning tool

A preliminary estimate depends on assumptions about area, quality, scope and site conditions. It becomes more reliable as drawings, specifications and quotations develop. A per-square-foot figure is difficult to compare unless the area basis and inclusions are the same. Ask what is assumed, excluded and still uncertain.

Control changes before committing

For each proposed change, record what it alters, its additional or reduced cost, any delay and who approved it. Check the effect on other trades. Moving a kitchen appliance may also change joinery, wiring, plumbing and delivery sequencing.

Protect priorities and a reserve

List essential outcomes first, such as functional layouts and reliable wet areas. Identify optional upgrades that can be reduced or deferred. Set contingency according to project uncertainty rather than assuming one percentage fits every job. Existing buildings with concealed conditions need particular care.

Before accepting a proposal, compare the same scope, understand payment milestones and ask how variations are valued. Agree professional fees and services directly with the appointed team; there is no fee promise in this guide.
